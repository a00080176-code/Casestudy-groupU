# First viewpoint analysis

## MATHEMATICAL VIEWPOINT ANALYSIS

### Introduction of the Analysis:

Looking from the perspective of the change from the traditional Nokia to the moderns smartphones like the Iphone , it shows how much mathematics has helped in the development of technology. Older phones were mainly used to make phone calls and text messages but smartphones combines communication , photography , internet access and computing in one single device. This big change can be explain through for example an exponential growth , binary numbers , algorithm , geometry and optimisation , etc.

#### 1º POINT: Exponential and Computing Growth

Exponential growth is a good example to explain how computing technology has improved has time passed. Intel explains that Moore’s law describes how the number of transistors on a computer chip in all his history has doubled in the gap of every two years ( Intel , 2025 ).


The mathematical model is:

**(P(t)=P_0\times2^{t/T})**

Here, (P_0) is the starting amount, (t) is the time passed and (T) is the doubling period.

If the amount doubles every two years, after ten years the calculation would be:

(P(10)=P_0\times2^5=32P_0)

This means that the amount would become 32 times larger using this model. However, this does not mean that a smartphone is actually 32 times father than a Nokia. It just proves how quickly certain aspects of technology can improve. These improvements in the development have helped modern phones go through more demanding applications and perform more difficult tasks.

#### 2º POINT: Binary numbers and Data Storage

Computers use binary numbers to store and process digital information. Binary only uses two digits: 0 and 1. According to the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), one byte contains eight bits.

For example, the binary number 1011 represents 11 in decimal:

(1011_(base 2)=8+2+1=11(base 10))

Nokia phones had limited storage compared with modern smartphones. They mainly stored contacts, messages and simple games, while today's phones can store thousands of photos, videos and applications.

#### 3º POINT:  Algorithms and Computational Complexity

Algorithms are instructions that tell a computer how to complete a task. Nokia phones used simple algorithms for games like Snake, such as moving the snake and checking when it hits an obstacule , leading to loosing and starting againg th game. Modern smartphones use more advanced algorithms for things like photography, navigation and artificial intelligence.

If the amount of work given is proportional to the square of 100 items, the calculation would be (100^2=10,000). This shows why choosing an efficient algorithm is important.

#### 4º POINT: Geometry and Touchscreens

Nokia phones used physical buttons, but when iPhone was launched out , they introduced a multi-touch screen in 2007 (Apple, 2007). This made it possible to interact directly with items on the screen.

#### 5º POINT: Optimisation and Battery Life

Optimisation means finding a good solution while working within certain limits. Smartphone designers need to balance the performance of the smartphone with the battery life, temperature and device size.

A simple mathematical would be:

(\max(P-\lambda E))

Here, (P) represents performance, (E) represents energy consumption and (\lambda) represents how important saving energy is in the model. This is an example of how optimisation can be represented mathematically, rather than the exact formula used by Apple.

The aim is to make phones work well without using too much battery power leading to the malfunction of the smartphone in a short period of time.

In conclusion, the development from Nokia phones to modern smarphones like iPhones shows how mathematics is used in everyday technology growth and development. Exponential growth helps explain improvements in computing hardware, binary numbers are used for digital storage, and algorithms allow phones to perform for more complicated tasks. Geometry makes touchscreen interaction possible and gives the user a better experience using the smartphone, while optimisation helps balance the performance and battery life of the smartphone. These examples show that mathematics has helped turn mobile phones from basic communication devices into smartphones with many functions.

# Second viewpoint analysis

## BUSINESS VIEWPOINT ANALYSIS 

### Introduction of the analysis:

The change from Nokia to smartphones like Iphone has changed a lot the mobile phone industry. In the early 2000s Nokia was the one that was leading the phone companies , selling their devices at different prices and costumers needs. But when Apple introduced to the market Iphone in 2007 , the market changed drastically. From a business point of view, this shows how important innovation , marketing , competition and understanding customers are.

#### 1º POINT: Competition and Market Share 

Before Iphone was introduced in the market , Nokia was the one that a major dominance in the mobile phone market. Its success came from offering good and reliable phones, a bigger range of models and products for different prices and budgets. According to researches that was published in the Business History , Nokia became the number one globally in mobile phones, but its position decreased as the smartphone market developed.

Apple caused a big change in the mobile phone market. Instead of focusing mainly on tradition mobile phones features , it combined a mobile phone , music player and a internet device into one product. Apple also introduced as an innovation the touchscreen interface. That changed the perspective of how people interacted with their phones.

This increased the competition in the mobile phone market because customers began expecting more from their devices. Nokia had to compete not only with the price of the market and responsibility but also on the software and user experience part. This demonstrates that business must adapt when competitors introduce products that change costumers expectations.


#### 2º POINT: Innovation and Business Strategy

Upgrades was an important factor in Apple’s success. The company focused on making the Iphone easy to use and connecting its hardware with software and services. The App Store and othe services helped make the Iphone to be more useful as time passed , giving customers more reasons to remain with the company of Apple.

Nokia also developed smartphones , like for example the N95 , but it struggled to compete with the changing of smartphones market. Research made by Vuori and Huy (2016) examines how Nokia lost its number one position in the market and explains the importance of management decisions and organisational problems during this period.

This competition proves that having a successful business in the pass does not assure success in the future. Companies needs to know and recognise changes in technology and customer preferences as time passes and respond effectively.


#### 3º POINT: Marketing and Customer Behaviour

Marketing was also an important factor in the competition between Nokia and Apple. Nokia sold many different models in the past , so customers could choose a phone that actually matched their preferences and budget. Apple first presented the Iphone as a luxury product and focused on its design, touchscreen and user experience. This helped Apple build a strong brand identity for the future. Customers were not just buying a phone as usual , they were joining Apple’s wider world of products ,devices and services , which could make them later loyal and more likely buy another Apple device.

#### 4º POINT: Revenue and Business Growth:

The smartphone industry also changed how companies earned their money. Old traditional mobile phone business mainly depended on selling their devices. Smartphones created more opportunities through the creation of applications , digital services and online platforms.

Apple business model allowed it to connect to Iphone with services like the App Store, creating additional ways to make more benefit beyond just selling hardware.

To conclude , the growth from Nokia mobile phones to the Iphone , clearly explains how competition  , innovation , marketing and business strategy in a company influence success in the market. For example , Nokia was highly successful in the past , but changes in the smartphone market created new challenges and there is where we see Apple step up , taking advantage of these changes by combining technology , design , software and services into one device , increasing highly the user experience.

# Conclusion – Synthesis and Broader Implications

Honestly, the jump from old Nokia phones to the iPhone is one of the strange, biggest changes in tech. A phone used to be something you grabbed to call your mum or send a quick text. Now it's your camera, your bank, your maps, your everything. Whichever way you look at it, history, business, maths, even ethics, smartphones did a lot more than help us chat. They changed how companies make money, how people live day to day, and what the tech industry even is.

Start with Nokia, because for a lot of people that was their first phone. Around the late 90s and early 2000s they were everywhere. The phones were cheap, you could drop them and they'd survive, and there was a model for pretty much anyone. Calls, texts, swapping the cover or the ringtone, that was all people needed back then. Texting really took off partly thanks to Nokia too. And then, pretty quickly, they lost it. Nokia is a good reminder that being on top doesn't mean you'll stay there, especially if you ignore where tech is heading.

Then 2007 happens and Apple shows the iPhone. Touchscreen, internet, music, messages, all in one thing. Before that you'd carry a phone and an iPod and maybe a camera. The App Store arrived in 2008 and that was huge, because suddenly anyone with an idea could build an app and make money from it. Phones turned into platforms, basically. Android did its bit as well, since loads of brands used it and had to compete, which pushed everything forward. Put Nokia next to Apple and the difference is obvious: one kept coming up with new stuff and paid attention to what people wanted, the other stuck with what had already worked. You can't just rely on past success. You have to keep changing as tech and people's habits do.

Business-wise, smartphones opened up stuff nobody had before. Phone companies used to make money by selling phones, simple as that. Now it's apps, subscriptions, ads, online services and plenty more. Even a tiny local shop can find customers through Instagram or sell online without a big store. It also made new jobs, like app developers, cybersecurity, digital marketing and tech support. So one new bit of technology can end up growing the economy and changing how whole companies run.

Maths is in all of this too, even if hardly anyone thinks about it. Exponential growth, binary, algorithms, geometry, optimisation, phones rely on all of it. Chips keep getting stronger, which is why your phone can handle things that needed a proper computer before. Binary is how it stores and reads everything. Algorithms run the maps, the camera tricks and face unlock. Geometry is part of how the touchscreen knows where your finger is. And optimisation is how engineers get decent speed without the battery dying in an hour. Maths isn't only something you suffer through in class, it's literally inside your pocket.

Socially, it's changed a lot. You can message someone, video call them or scroll social media from anywhere, which is great. It also made school stuff, news, banking and directions way easier. But it isn't all good. Too much screen time and feeling like you can't put your phone down can cause problems with your mental health, and younger people get it worst. Privacy is another concern aswell, because phones keeps the track of your location and what you do online. Then there's e-waste, since making and binning phones all the time hurts the environment. So yeah, progress has a price.

Looking ahead, phone companies need to take more responsibility. Make phones that last longer, are easier to repair and can actually be recycled. Look after our data properly and let us choose what happens with it. Governments and companies should work together aswell, so people from poorer backgrounds they don't stay left out from the new upgrades and innovation. And people, should try also to use mobile phones in a healthier way.

To sum up, the story from Nokia to the iPhone shows how fast technology can change and how much it affects everyone. Nokia made phones normal. Then Apple and the rest turned them into devices that do loads and connect people, businesses and online services. It's not just better technology either, it changed jobs, education, how we talk and how we act. There are real problems with privacy, the environment and well-being, but I still think smartphones have been mostly good for society. For me the big lesson is that doing well in tech isn't only about having new ideas, you've also got to adapt responsibly to what people need. As phones keep developing, the hard part will be making sure new upgrades helps people without causing too many problems for them in any way.
