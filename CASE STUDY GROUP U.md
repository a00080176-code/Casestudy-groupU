# First viewpoint analysis

## MATHEMATICAL VIEWPOINT ANALYSIS

### Introduction of the Analysis:

Looking from the perspective of the change from the traditional Nokia to the moderns smartphones like the Iphone , it shows how much mathematics has helped in the development of technology. Older phones were mainly used to make phone calls and text messages but smartphones combines communication , photography , internet access and computing in one single device. This big change can be explain through for example an exponential growth , binary numbers , algorithm , geometry and optimisation , etc.

#### 1º POINT: Exponential and Computing Growth

Exponential growth is a good example to explain how computing technology has improved has time passed. Intel explains that Moore’s law describes how the number of transistors on a computer chip in all his history has doubled in the gap of every two years ( Intel , 2025 ).


The mathematical model is:

**(P(t)=P_0\times2^{t/T})**

Here, (P_0) is the starting amount, (t) is the time passed and (T) is the doubling period.

If the amount doubles every two years, after ten years the calculation would be:

(P(10)=P_0\times2^5=32P_0)

This means that the amount would become 32 times larger using this model. However, this does not mean that a smartphone is actually 32 times father than a Nokia. It just proves how quickly certain aspects of technology can improve. These improvements in the development have helped modern phones go through more demanding applications and perform more difficult tasks.

#### 2º POINT: Binary numbers and Data Storage

Computers use binary numbers to store and process digital information. Binary only uses two digits: 0 and 1. According to the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), one byte contains eight bits.

For example, the binary number 1011 represents 11 in decimal:

(1011_(base 2)=8+2+1=11(base 10))

Nokia phones had limited storage compared with modern smartphones. They mainly stored contacts, messages and simple games, while today's phones can store thousands of photos, videos and applications.

#### 3º POINT:  Algorithms and Computational Complexity

Algorithms are instructions that tell a computer how to complete a task. Nokia phones used simple algorithms for games like Snake, such as moving the snake and checking when it hits an obstacule , leading to loosing and starting againg th game. Modern smartphones use more advanced algorithms for things like photography, navigation and artificial intelligence.

If the amount of work given is proportional to the square of 100 items, the calculation would be (100^2=10,000). This shows why choosing an efficient algorithm is important.

#### 4º POINT: Geometry and Touchscreens

Nokia phones used physical buttons, but when iPhone was launched out , they introduced a multi-touch screen in 2007 (Apple, 2007). This made it possible to interact directly with items on the screen.

#### 5º POINT: Optimisation and Battery Life

Optimisation means finding a good solution while working within certain limits. Smartphone designers need to balance the performance of the smartphone with the battery life, temperature and device size.

A simple mathematical would be:

(\max(P-\lambda E))

Here, (P) represents performance, (E) represents energy consumption and (\lambda) represents how important saving energy is in the model. This is an example of how optimisation can be represented mathematically, rather than the exact formula used by Apple.

The aim is to make phones work well without using too much battery power leading to the malfunction of the smartphone in a short period of time.

In conclusion, the development from Nokia phones to modern smarphones like iPhones shows how mathematics is used in everyday technology growth and development. Exponential growth helps explain improvements in computing hardware, binary numbers are used for digital storage, and algorithms allow phones to perform for more complicated tasks. Geometry makes touchscreen interaction possible and gives the user a better experience using the smartphone, while optimisation helps balance the performance and battery life of the smartphone. These examples show that mathematics has helped turn mobile phones from basic communication devices into smartphones with many functions.

# Second viewpoint analysis

## BUSINESS VIEWPOINT ANALYSIS 

### Introduction of the analysis:

The change from Nokia phones to smartphones like the iPhone has transformed the mobile phone industry. In the early 2000s Nokia was one of the leading phone companies, selling devices for different prices and customer needs. But when Apple introduced the iPhone in 2007, the market began to change. From a business point of view, this shows how important innovation, marketing, competition and understanding customers are.

#### 1º POINT: Competition and Market Share 

Before the iPhone was introduced, Nokia was a major player in the mobile phone market. Its success came from offering reliable phones, a wide range of models and products for different budgets. According to research published in Business History, Nokia had become a global leader in mobile phones, but its position weakened as the smartphone market developed.

Apple entered the market with a different approach. Instead of focusing mainly on traditional phone features, it combined a phone, music player and internet device into one product. Apple also introduced a touchscreen interface that changed how people interacted with their phones (Apple, 2007).

This increased competition because customers began expecting more from their devices. Nokia had to compete not only on price and reliability but also on software and user experience. This shows that businesses must adapt when competitors introduce products that change customer expectations.

#### 2º POINT: Innovation and Business Strategy

Innovation was an important factor in Apple's success. The company focused on making the iPhone easy to use and connecting its hardware with software and services. The App Store and other services helped make the iPhone more useful over time, giving customers additional reasons to remain with Apple.

Nokia also developed smartphones, such as the N95, but it struggled to compete with the changing smartphone market. Research by Vuori and Huy (2016) examines how Nokia lost its leading position and explains the importance of management decisions and organisational challenges during this period.

This comparison shows that having a successful business in the past does not guarantee future success. Companies need to recognise changes in technology and customer preferences and respond effectively.

#### 3º POINT: Marketing and Customer Behaviour

Marketing also played an important role in the competition between Nokia and Apple. Nokia offered many different models, allowing customers to choose phones based on their budget and needs. Apple initially positioned the iPhone as a premium product, focusing on its design, touchscreen and overall user experience.

Apple's strategy helped create a strong brand identity. Customers were not only buying a phone; they were also buying into Apple's wider range of products and services. This could encourage customer loyalty and make people more likely to purchase another Apple device in the future.

#### 4º POINT: Revenue and Business Growth:

The smartphone industry also changed how companies earned money. Traditional mobile phone businesses mainly depended on selling devices. Smartphones created further opportunities through applications, digital services and online platforms.

Apple's business model allowed it to connect the iPhone with services such as the App Store, creating additional ways to generate revenue beyond selling hardware.

In conclusion, the evolution from Nokia phones to the iPhone shows how competition, innovation, marketing and business strategy influence success. Nokia was highly successful, but changes in the smartphone market created new challenges. Apple took advantage of these changes by combining technology, design, software and services into one connected experience.

Smartphones have opened up many new opportunities for businesses. 

Now, companies can sell products online, advertise with mobile apps, and connect with customers more easily.

For example, small businesses can use platforms like Instagram, TikTok, and online shopping sites to promote their products and reach customers in other countries , and this has become a huge platform for new entrepreneurs.

### Negative Impacts 
 
Privacy and Personal Information

Modern smartphones gather personal details like your location, browsing history, and online activity. 

Companies use this information to improve their services and show you personalised ads, but this can lead to privacy concerns.

Companies need to keep user information safe and clearly explain how they use it. Apple (n.d.) points out how important it is to protect user privacy.

Environmental Impact and Electronic Waste

Smartphones add to electronic waste because people often get new phones even when their old ones still work. Making phones also uses up energy and natural resources, which can hurt the environment.

According to the World Health Organization (2024), around 62 million tonnes of electronic waste were produced worldwide in 2022.

Companies should support recycling, repairs, and making phones that last longer to help reduce harm to the environment.

# Conclusion – Synthesis and Broader Implications

To finish, the evolution of mobile phones from Nokia's traditional devices to modern smartphones such as Apple's iPhone represents one of the biggest changes in modern technology. Mobile phones have developed from simple communication devices into powerful tools that influence almost every part of everyday life. By looking at this development from historical, business, social, mathematical and ethical viewpoints, it becomes clear that smartphones have not only changed the way people communicate but have also transformed businesses, society and the technology industry as a whole.

Looking back at the Nokia era, it is clear that Nokia played an important role in making mobile phones accessible and popular around the world. During the late 1990s and early 2000s, Nokia became one of the most successful mobile phone manufacturers because of its reliable devices, affordable prices and wide range of models. Features such as calling, texting and customisable phone designs helped make mobile phones an important part of people's daily lives. Nokia also contributed to the development of mobile communication by helping make text messaging a normal way for people to stay connected. However, Nokia's decline demonstrates that even successful companies can lose their position when they fail to adapt to major technological changes.

The introduction of Apple's iPhone in 2007 marked a turning point in the mobile phone industry. Instead of focusing mainly on traditional phone features, Apple combined a touchscreen, internet access, music and communication into one device. The introduction of the App Store in 2008 further changed the industry by creating opportunities for developers and businesses to produce and sell applications. This helped transform smartphones into platforms rather than simply devices. Google's Android operating system also contributed to this transformation by allowing different manufacturers to compete in the growing smartphone market. The comparison between Nokia and Apple demonstrates the importance of innovation, business strategy and understanding customer expectations. A company cannot depend entirely on its previous success; it must continue developing its products and adapting to changes in technology and consumer behaviour.

From a business perspective, the smartphone revolution created new industries and opportunities. In the past, mobile phone companies mainly earned money through selling physical devices. Today, the industry also generates revenue through applications, subscriptions, digital services, advertising and online platforms. Smartphones have allowed businesses of all sizes to reach customers through social media, mobile applications and online shopping. They have also created employment opportunities in areas such as software development, cybersecurity, digital marketing and technology support. This demonstrates how a technological development can create economic growth and change the way companies operate.

Mathematics has also played an important role in this transformation. The development of modern smartphones depends on mathematical concepts such as exponential growth, binary numbers, algorithms, geometry and optimisation. Improvements in computing power have allowed smartphones to perform increasingly complex tasks, while binary numbers make it possible to store and process digital information. Algorithms help applications perform tasks such as navigation, photography and facial recognition. Geometry has contributed to touchscreen technology, allowing users to interact directly with their devices. Furthermore, optimisation helps engineers balance performance, battery life and energy consumption. These examples show that mathematics is not only a theoretical subject but also an essential part of the technology people use every day.

From a social perspective, smartphones have made communication faster and more convenient. People can now communicate through instant messaging, video calls and social media regardless of their location. Smartphones have also improved access to education, information, banking, navigation and other everyday services. However, these benefits have introduced challenges. Excessive screen time, dependence on technology and the effect of constant online activity on people's well-being are important concerns, particularly among younger generations. Privacy is another major issue because smartphones collect personal information, including location data and online activity. In addition, the production and disposal of smartphones contribute to electronic waste and environmental damage. These problems demonstrate that technological progress does not always come without negative consequences.

Looking towards the future, smartphone companies have an important responsibility to make technology more sustainable, secure and accessible. Manufacturers should focus on producing devices that last longer, are easier to repair and can be recycled more effectively. They should also improve data protection and give users greater control over their personal information. Governments and technology companies should work together to reduce digital inequality and ensure that people from different backgrounds can benefit from technological developments. At the same time, individuals should be encouraged to develop healthier relationships with their devices.

In conclusion, the journey from Nokia to the iPhone demonstrates how quickly technology can change and how strongly it can influence society. Nokia helped establish mobile phones as an essential part of everyday life, while Apple and other smartphone manufacturers transformed them into multifunctional devices that connect people, businesses and digital services. The wider implications go beyond technological improvements because this evolution has changed economic opportunities, communication, education and social behaviour. Although smartphones have created challenges involving privacy, environmental sustainability and well-being, their overall contribution to modern society has been significant. Ultimately, the most important lesson from this evolution is that technological success depends not only on innovation but also on the ability to adapt responsibly to the needs of society. As smartphones continue to develop, the challenge will be to ensure that future technological advancements improve people's lives while minimising their negative effects.
